# Captain

The Captain runs the session from the first word to the last. It sets up the agenda, opens the session, introduces each person and each part, keeps things moving and on time, and closes at the end. When it is done well, everyone else can concentrate on their own role and the session feels like one event, not a list of separate exercises.

**Filled by:** a human or AI.

The Captain is not the same as the room host. The room host is the person whose computer the room runs on. The Captain is a session role that anyone in the room, or AI, can take.

## What the Captain does

### Before the session

- **Builds the agenda.** Chooses which parts the session will have, in what order, and how long each should take. The default agenda is below.
- **Sets or confirms the theme.** The theme shapes the impromptu questions, the Power Word, and the Captain's own remarks.
- **Fills the roles.** People in the room pick the roles they want. Every role left open goes to AI, and the Pacer and Spotter always run in code. No session is blocked because a role is empty.
- **Checks that everyone is ready.** Each person knows their role, the speaker has entered a title, target length, and goals, and microphones have passed the setup check.

### Opening

- **Welcomes everyone and introduces the theme** in a few sentences.
- **Introduces each role.** The Pacer, Spotter, and Form Checker each explain briefly what they will do. The Form Checker presents the Power Word.

### During the session

- **Introduces each speaker.** Uses the speaker's own introduction when they provided one, and gives the speech title.
- **Bridges between parts.** After each part, one or two sentences that respond to what just happened and set up what comes next.
- **Hands over and takes back control.** The Sparring Partner runs the sparring round and the coach gives the evaluation. The Captain introduces them and picks the session back up afterward.
- **Watches the overall clock.** The Pacer tracks each speaker. The Captain tracks the whole session and shortens later parts when earlier ones run long.
- **Adapts to what happens.** See "When things change" below.

### Closing

- **Calls for the reports.** Pacer, Spotter, and Form Checker, in that order.
- **Thanks everyone who took part**, with a specific word for guests and first-time speakers.
- **Ends the session.** After this, the session record is saved for each participant.

## Default agenda

| Part | Led by | Typical length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening and theme | Captain | 1 minute |
| Role introductions and Power Word | Captain, with each role | 2 minutes |
| Prepared speech | Speaker | 5 to 7 minutes |
| Sparring round | Sparring Partner | 5 to 10 minutes |
| Speech evaluation | Coach | 2 to 3 minutes |
| Reports | Pacer, Spotter, Form Checker | 3 minutes |
| Closing | Captain | 1 minute |

Any part can be removed. A session can be a prepared speech with an evaluation and nothing else, or a sparring round on its own. With more than one prepared speaker, the speech and evaluation parts repeat.

## When things change

| What happens | What the Captain does |
| --- | --- |
| A person holding a role drops out or loses their connection | Hands the role to AI and continues. If the person returns, they can take it back at the next break between parts. |
| The speaker drops out mid-speech | Pauses the session and waits. If they do not return, moves to the sparring round or closes. |
| Someone joins late | Welcomes them at the next break between parts and offers an open role. |
| The session is running long | Cuts questions from the sparring round first, then shortens the transitions. Speeches and evaluations are never cut short. |
| Nobody volunteers in the sparring round | Works with the Sparring Partner to invite people directly, or ends the round early. |

## Solo practice

When one person practices alone, AI is always the Captain. Two styles are available:

- **Full session.** The complete agenda with introductions and transitions, for the experience of a real meeting.
- **Quick practice.** The Captain gives a one-line introduction, and the session goes straight to the speech and then the feedback. This suits a rehearsal between other tasks.

## When AI takes the role

- **Code controls the order, and the language model writes the words.** The agenda is a fixed sequence managed by the app, so the session can never skip a part or get stuck. The model only produces what the Captain says at each step.
- **Its transitions refer to what was really said.** It works from the transcripts, so a bridge after a speech mentions something from that speech.
- **It stays brief.** Each remark is a few sentences. The session belongs to the speakers.
- **It waits for humans.** It never talks over someone, and it gives a human role holder time to start before prompting them.
- **It cannot sense the mood of the room.** A human Captain can see energy dropping and respond to it. The AI works from what it can measure: the clock, who has spoken, and who is connected.

## What the Captain practices

This section applies to humans taking the role.

- **Organization.** Preparing an agenda and making sure every role is covered before the start.
- **Facilitation.** Leading a group through a structured session without dominating it.
- **Time management.** Keeping the whole session on schedule, not only one's own part.
- **Speaking as a host.** Introductions, transitions, and thanks are short speeches of their own, mostly improvised.
- **Adaptability.** Staying calm and adjusting when a role is empty, a connection fails, or the schedule slips.

## What the Captain receives

- The theme, the list of participants, and the roles each person chose
- Each speaker's title, target length, and introduction
- The live session, plus the running clock
- For an AI Captain: transcripts of each part as it finishes, and connection status for each participant

## What the Captain produces

- The agenda and the role assignments
- The spoken opening, introductions, transitions, and closing
- Decisions during the session: reassigning roles, shortening parts, pausing, and ending
