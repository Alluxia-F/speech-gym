# Sparring Partner

The Sparring Partner runs the sparring round, the impromptu part of the session. It throws out questions nobody has seen in advance, and each person who answers gets one to two minutes to think on their feet. The aim is to make unprepared speaking feel normal: answering a question in a meeting, an interview, or a conversation without freezing.

**Filled by:** a human or AI.

## What the Sparring Partner does

### Before the round

- **Prepares questions around the session theme.** A typical round has three to five questions. Solo practice can run as few as one or as many as the speaker wants.
- **Fits the questions to the room.** Easier, more personal questions for newer speakers, and more abstract or challenging ones for experienced speakers.
- **Keeps the questions secret.** Nobody who will answer sees them beforehand. When AI holds the role, the questions are generated at the start of the round and are not shown until asked.

### Opening the round

- **Explains how it works, briefly.** The round is practice for speaking without preparation, each answer should last one to two minutes, and the Pacer's signals show when to wrap up. There is no wrong answer.

### During the round

- **Asks one question at a time.** The question is spoken aloud and shown on screen. The clock starts when the person begins to answer.
- **Chooses who answers:**
  - In solo practice, the speaker answers every question.
  - In a room with friends, the Sparring Partner asks for a volunteer first. If nobody volunteers, it invites someone who has not spoken much in this session.
  - Anyone can pass, and guests are never put on the spot.
- **Listens to each answer and responds to it.** After each answer, one or two sentences that pick up something the person actually said, then on to the next question. This is a bridge, not an evaluation.
- **Keeps the round moving.** Short introductions to each question, no long commentary, and an eye on the total time.

### Closing the round

- **Wraps up in a sentence or two** and hands back to the Captain.

## What makes a good question

- **Open-ended.** It cannot be answered with yes or no.
- **Answerable by anyone.** It draws on experience and opinion, not specialist knowledge.
- **Short.** One sentence that can be understood on first hearing.
- **Connected to the theme**, but from a different angle each time.
- **Varied in type** across the round:

| Type | Example for the theme "travel" |
| --- | --- |
| Personal story | "Tell us about a trip that did not go as planned." |
| Opinion | "Is it better to travel alone or with others? Why?" |
| Hypothetical | "You can take one more trip in your life. Where do you go?" |
| Persuasion | "Convince us to visit your hometown." |
| Object or image | "Describe the one item you never travel without, and what it says about you." |

- **Safe for the room.** Nothing that pushes people to share private matters or take sides on divisive issues, unless the group chose that theme on purpose.

## What happens to each answer

Every answer is a short speech, so the other roles treat it as one:

- The Pacer runs the one to two minute signals.
- The Spotter logs fillers and pauses.
- The Form Checker includes it in the language report.
- In solo practice, the AI coach gives a brief evaluation of each answer: one thing that worked and one thing to try.

## When AI takes the role

- **It writes fresh questions each session** from the theme, and avoids repeating questions the speaker has had before.
- **It adjusts as the round goes on.** If answers are running short or the speaker is struggling, the next question gets easier. If they are comfortable, it gets harder.
- **Its response to each answer comes from the transcript**, so it refers to what was really said.
- **It can only invite people fairly, not read their mood.** A human Sparring Partner can see who looks ready and who looks anxious. The AI works from simpler signals: who volunteered, who has spoken least, and who has passed.

## What the Sparring Partner practices

This section applies to humans taking the role.

- **Creative planning.** Writing questions that suit this particular group, its mood, and its range of experience.
- **Engaging delivery.** Presenting a question so that people want to answer it.
- **Reading the room.** Sensing who is ready for a challenge and who needs an easier start.
- **Facilitation.** Keeping a multi-person segment on time and flowing without taking it over.
- **Listening and responding on the spot.** Summing up or building on an answer in a sentence, with no time to prepare.

## What the Sparring Partner receives

- The session theme
- Who is in the room, their roles, and their experience level when known
- How many questions to ask, or how long the round should last
- For an AI Sparring Partner: the transcript of each answer, and the speaker's past questions

## What the Sparring Partner produces

- A short explanation of the round at its start
- The questions, spoken and shown one at a time
- A brief response after each answer
- A record of the round: each question, who answered it, and the answer's transcript
