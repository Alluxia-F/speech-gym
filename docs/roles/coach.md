# Coach

The coach helps the speaker get better. The job is to describe what worked, what held the speech back, and what to try next time. It is not to hand out a grade or a verdict. A good evaluation leaves the speaker wanting to give the speech again.

**Filled by:** a human or AI.

## What the coach does

### Before the speech

- **Reads the speaker's goals.** The speaker sets one to three goals for the attempt. These decide what the coach pays most attention to.
- **Checks the basics of the speech.** Title, target length, and whether this is a first attempt or a repeat. On a repeat, the coach also looks at what was suggested last time.

### During the speech

- **Listens to the whole speech without interrupting.**
- **Notes specific moments.** A line that landed, a transition that was hard to follow, a section that felt rushed. Notes point to exact words or times, so the feedback can quote them later.
- **Covers the main areas:**

| Area | What to look at | AI coach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | Opening, flow between points, close | Yes, from the transcript |
| Content | Clarity of the message, stories, examples | Yes, from the transcript |
| Language | Word choice, repetition, vivid phrasing | Yes, from the transcript |
| Use of time | Length against the target, balance between sections | Yes, from measured timing |
| Pace and pauses | Speed, rushed sections, pauses used for effect | Yes, from measured timing |
| Vocal variety | Changes in pitch, volume, and energy | Planned for a later version |
| Gestures and eye contact | Body language and connection with the audience | Human coaches only, in rooms with video |

An AI coach says plainly which areas it could not observe, so the speaker knows what the feedback does and does not cover.

### After the speech

- **Gives a spoken evaluation of two to three minutes.** Short enough to stay focused and easy to remember.
- **Opens with what worked.** Names real strengths and quotes the moments that showed them. General praise such as "good job" does not count.
- **Offers two or three suggestions.** Each one points to a specific moment, explains why it matters, and says what to try instead. More than three is too many to act on.
- **Answers the speaker's goals directly.** For each goal, the coach says whether it was met and what the evidence was.
- **Closes with encouragement.** Notes progress since earlier attempts when there is any, and what the speaker is closest to getting right.
- **Leaves a written summary.** The same points in short form, saved with the session so the speaker can return to them.

## Ground rules

- **Evaluate the speech, not the person.** Comment on what was said and how, never on who the speaker is.
- **Be specific.** If a comment could apply to any speech, replace it with one that could only apply to this one.
- **Be honest.** Praise that is not earned teaches nothing. If a goal was missed, say so, kindly and clearly.
- **Speak as one listener.** Phrases such as "I noticed" and "I lost the thread when" describe one person's experience. They are not rulings.
- **Fit the speaker.** A first-time speaker and an experienced one need different depth and different tone.

## What the coach practices

This section applies to humans taking the role. Evaluating is a skill of its own, and many people find it improves their own speaking.

- **Focused listening.** Holding attention on someone else's speech from the first word to the last.
- **Observation.** Catching the specific detail that explains why a moment worked or did not.
- **Analysis.** Working out the cause behind an impression, such as why the middle section dragged.
- **Giving feedback people can use.** Saying something hard in a way the listener can accept and act on.
- **Thinking on a deadline.** Organizing notes into a clear short talk within minutes.
- **Reflective writing.** Turning observations into a brief written summary that still makes sense a week later.

## What the coach receives

- The speaker's title, target length, and goals
- The live speech, by audio and by video when the room has it
- For an AI coach: the transcript with timestamps, and the measured pace, pauses, and timing
- Evaluations from earlier attempts at the same speech, when there are any

## What the coach produces

- A spoken evaluation of two to three minutes
- A written summary: strengths, two or three suggestions, and a result for each of the speaker's goals
