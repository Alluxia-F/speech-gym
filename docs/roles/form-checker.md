# Form Checker

The Form Checker pays attention to the language itself: the words speakers choose and how they put sentences together. It sets a small vocabulary challenge for the session, notes language that worked well and language that slipped, and reports at the end so everyone leaves with a slightly larger and sharper vocabulary.

**Filled by:** a human or AI.

## What the Form Checker does

### At the start of the session

- **Introduces the Power Word.** The Form Checker picks one word that fits the session's theme, gives its meaning, and uses it in an example sentence. Everyone who speaks is challenged to work it into what they say.
- **Chooses a word worth learning.** A good pick is useful in everyday speech, not too obscure, and flexible enough to fit different topics. Speakers can also bring their own word.

### During the session

- **Listens to every speaker.** This includes the prepared speech, impromptu answers, and the other role holders.
- **Notes language that worked.** A vivid image, a well-chosen verb, a memorable phrase, or a rhetorical device such as a contrast or a list of three. Exact words are written down so they can be quoted.
- **Notes language that slipped:**

| What to listen for | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar slips | "There's many reasons" in place of "There are many reasons" |
| Sentences left unfinished | "And the reason I wanted to, well, anyway, the next day..." |
| Word mix-ups | "It had a big affect on me" in place of "effect" |
| Vague or overused words | "thing", "stuff", "very", "really" doing the work of a more exact word |
| Wordiness | "at this point in time" in place of "now" |

- **Tracks the Power Word.** Who used it, how many times, and whether it was used correctly.

### At the end of the session

- **Gives a short report.** About one to two minutes, covering:
  - Who used the Power Word
  - The best language of the session, quoted and credited to the speaker
  - Two or three slips worth learning from, each with a better version
- **Leaves written notes for each speaker.** Their own highlights and slips, saved with the session.

## Ground rules

- **Judge it as speech, not as writing.** Spoken language is looser than written language. Contractions, short fragments used for effect, and a conversational style are fine. Only note what would distract or confuse a listener.
- **Praise first, and as specifically as you correct.** The role is about growing vocabulary as much as catching errors.
- **Pick the few that matter.** Listing every slip is discouraging and hard to remember. Choose the ones that recur or that change the meaning.
- **Always offer the better version.** A correction without an alternative does not help.
- **Respect how people speak.** Accent, dialect, and regional phrasing are not errors. For someone speaking a second language, focus on the slips that affect clarity.

## How this role differs from its neighbors

- **Spotter** counts fillers and pauses. The Form Checker leaves those alone.
- **Coach** assesses one speech as a whole against the speaker's goals. The Form Checker covers only language, but for everyone who speaks in the session.

## When AI takes the role

- **It works from the transcript.** The AI Form Checker reads what the speech-to-text model produced, with the Power Word counted by code.
- **It allows for transcription errors.** A wrong word in the transcript may be the transcriber's mistake, not the speaker's. This is most likely with words that sound alike. The AI Form Checker only reports a slip when the surrounding words make it clear what was said, and marks uncertain cases as uncertain.
- **It picks the Power Word from the theme.** It avoids words used in the speaker's recent sessions, and matches the difficulty to the speaker's level.
- **It works in the language of the session.** Word lists, examples, and the report follow the language being practiced.

## What the Form Checker practices

This section applies to humans taking the role.

- **A wider vocabulary.** Choosing and explaining a new word each session adds up quickly.
- **Listening for detail.** Hearing how something was said, not only what was said.
- **Explaining clearly.** Introducing a word so that people can use it a minute later.
- **Judgment.** Deciding which few observations are worth the room's time.
- **Tactful feedback.** Correcting someone's language in front of others without making them self-conscious.

## What the Form Checker receives

- The session theme, used to pick the Power Word
- Every speaking slot in the session, live
- For an AI Form Checker: the transcripts, and the Power Word count
- Each speaker's recent Power Words, to avoid repeats

## What the Form Checker produces

- The Power Word, with its meaning and an example, at the start of the session
- A spoken report of one to two minutes at the end
- Written notes for each speaker: highlights, slips with better versions, and Power Word use
